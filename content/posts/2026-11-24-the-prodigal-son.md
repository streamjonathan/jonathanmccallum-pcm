*Di Return of di Prodigal Son, Rembrandt van Rijn (c. 1666–1669), public domain.*

Most of us know how e be to walk away — and to wonder if we fit come back. Hello to evribodi wey dey read for Nigeria — I glad say you dey here.

One man get two sons. Di younger talk, "Papa, give me my share of di property." So di papa divide am between dem. Soon after, di younger son go far country waste am all. Den famine come, and im begin starve.

Im come to imsef: "I go go back to my papa." But while im still far off, im papa see am, run go meet am, kiss am. "Papa, I don sin against heaven and before you," di son talk. But di papa talk, "Bring di best robe. Put ring for im hand. Make we chop and celebrate. My son don die before, and now im alive again. Im don lost, and now dem don find am."

Di older broda vex — im don stay work all those years. But di papa go out to am too. "Son, you dey always with me. All wetin be mine na yours. But we must celebrate — your broda don lost, and now dem don find am."

Wich broda you see for yourself? And e get road home wey you don dey postpone?

If you dey read dis for Nigeria — or anywhere for di world where you read Pidgin — abeg, you fit take small time greet Jonathan?

*Di Bible words for here na dem translate am for dis site.*
